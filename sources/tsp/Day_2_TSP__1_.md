When you buy a cup of milk tea, there are actually only three questions in your mind:

✅  “Is this cup worth waiting in line for ten minutes?” → That’s value recognition (V)

✅     “28 yuan? The same one next door is only 22, why is this one more expensive?” → That’s price recognition (P)

✅  “What if it doesn’t taste good? I’ll have wasted both my time and money.” → That’s risk recognition (R)
When buying an umbrella during the rainy season, these are the user's doubts

✅  “A typhoon is coming, will this umbrella hold up?” → That’s value recognition (V)

“The umbrella ribs are made of aerospace aluminum, resistant to level 8 winds—just now, three
umbrellas nearby flipped over in the gust, but this one didn’t budge.”

✅  “99? Why is it so expensive?” → That’s price recognition (P)

“Same model as Sam’s Club, always 129, but today only 99—save 30 yuan, enough for
two cups of hot coffee to warm you up.”

✅  “It sounds good, but what if it breaks?” → That’s risk recognition (R)

It’s going to rain continuously starting tomorrow. If your umbrella breaks, come find me. Not only will I replace it with a new one, I’ll even
compensate you for your trouble.

The higher the V, the more users expect.

The lower the P, the more users expect.

The less R, the more reassured users feel.
